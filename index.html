AI Use in Higher Education

Why AI use is a detriment to student learning in Academia.

Op-Ed

In this story

The Nitty Gritty In Summary

L2-L3 · Flow and Positioned Relationships

Artificial intelligence has become increasingly common in academic environments, changing the way students approach assignments, research, and studying. Tools such as ChatGPT can generate essays, answer questions, solve mathematical problems, and explain complicated subjects in a matter of seconds. Although these tools can be convenient, their growing presence in education raises concerns about how they affect students' ability to learn. Education is meant to help individuals develop critical thinking, problem-solving, creativity, and independent learning skills. When students rely too heavily on artificial intelligence to complete their academic work, they risk missing out on the very experiences that help them develop these abilities. While AI can be useful in certain situations, its overuse in academia can negatively affect student learning by encouraging dependency, weakening critical thinking skills, and reducing the value of education.

One of the biggest concerns with AI in education is that it encourages students to become dependent on technology rather than developing their own abilities. Assignments are designed to challenge students and encourage them to apply what they have learned. However, when students use AI to generate answers or complete assignments for them, they bypass the learning process entirely. Instead of struggling through a difficult problem, researching a topic, or organizing their thoughts, students can simply enter a prompt and receive an almost immediate response. Although this saves time, it prevents students from gaining valuable experience through practice. Over time, students may become less confident in their ability to complete work independently. This creates a problem because students will eventually encounter situations in their careers and personal lives where AI is unavailable or inappropriate to use. Without the knowledge and skills they were supposed to develop in school, they may struggle to handle these challenges on their own.

[AI] prevents students from gaining valuable experience through practice.

Another major disadvantage of AI in academia is its potential to weaken critical thinking and problem-solving skills. Learning is not simply about obtaining the correct answer; it is about understanding how and why that answer is correct. Students develop critical thinking by evaluating information, considering different perspectives, identifying mistakes, and forming their own conclusions. When AI does most of this work, students lose opportunities to practice these important skills. Additionally, AI-generated information is not always accurate. These tools can produce misleading information, incorrect explanations, or fabricated sources while presenting their responses confidently. Students who accept AI-generated answers without questioning or verifying them may develop poor research habits and have difficulty distinguishing reliable information from misinformation. Rather than becoming better researchers and independent thinkers, students risk becoming dependent on information that they do not fully understand.

AI use in education can also reduce the personal value of academic achievement. Completing an assignment provides students with an opportunity to demonstrate their knowledge, recognize their weaknesses, and improve their understanding of a subject. When students submit work that was largely generated by AI, their grades may no longer accurately represent their abilities or understanding. This creates an unfair situation for students who put genuine effort into completing their assignments. It also makes it more difficult for instructors to determine whether their students are actually learning the material. Grades should represent a student's understanding and effort, not simply their ability to use technology to produce an acceptable response. If students consistently rely on AI to complete their work, academic achievements may become less meaningful because they no longer reflect the knowledge students have gained.

L4-L5 · Layers, Shapes, and Fallback

Ultimately, the purpose of education is to prepare students for the challenges they will face throughout their lives. Artificial intelligence may provide convenience, but convenience should not come at the expense of meaningful learning. Students need opportunities to struggle, make mistakes, ask questions, and discover solutions independently. These experiences build confidence, knowledge, and essential skills that cannot be developed by simply copying answers from a computer. Although AI can serve as a helpful supplemental resource, it should never replace the effort and independent thinking required to receive an education. By limiting dependence on artificial intelligence, academic institutions can help ensure that students are genuinely learning, developing valuable skills, and preparing themselves for success beyond the classroom.
